How it picks a time

The service looks at your hours today and tomorrow in 15-minute slots, drops the slots you can't or don't want to run in, scores the rest on the weather and picks the best stretch as long as your run.

1. The forecast

Open-Meteo's hourly forecast for your start point, rounded to about 1 km and refreshed every hour. A slot takes the feels-like temperature and wind speed of the hour it falls in, and the rain and gusts forecast for that hour.

2. Slots that drop out

  • Slots that have already started, and slots outside your hours.
  • Slots outside sunrise to sunset, unless Dark is OK.
  • Slots whose chance of rain is above your Rain setting, or with 0.2 mm of rain an hour or more.

3. Scoring what is left

Each slot gets penalty points; lower is better. A window's score is the average of its slots.

  • Wind: 1 point per km/h above 12 km/h, plus half a point per km/h of gusts above 30.
  • Temperature: 1 point per degree the feels-like temperature is outside 6–16 °C.
  • Rain: 1 point per 20 % chance of rain.

Wind usually decides: at 30 km/h it outweighs even a certain shower.

Mind most picks the one that bothers you most: its points count three times. It doesn't set a limit, because the forecast decides what a day can offer; on a stormy day Wind still gets you the calmest time there is. Balanced counts all three the same. The Rain slider is the one hard limit.

4. The pick

The lowest score wins; on a tie, the earliest. A window never spans two hour ranges. With a route, the run's length is its distance at your pace, rounded up to 15 minutes; up to 3 hours.

When no hour-long window is dry enough, the page shows the least bad hour instead and says so: rain no longer rules slots out, but its chance weighs ten times as much and the amount counts too.

5. The wind line

It gives the window's average wind direction. Start into it, so it is behind you on the way home. With a loop it says whether to run it as drawn or reversed, from A to B whether it is a head- or tailwind, and under 10 km/h it just says little wind.

These weights are a starting point and get tuned on real runs. The timeline's shading shows each slot's score.

The API

Give it a location and the hours you can run. It returns the best weather window for each session length, today and tomorrow.

Example request

GET https://besttimetorun.com/api/window?lat=52.37&lon=4.89&avail=07-09,12-14,17-21

lat and lon are required. avail, dark, rain, prefer, lang and slots are optional; prefer (balanced, wind, rain or temp) makes that one count three times; slots=1 adds the day's 15-minute slots, which this page draws. The full contract is in openapi.yaml.

Capacity

The service accepts at most 300 locations per UTC day, to stay within the weather provider's free limits. A location that was admitted keeps working all day. A new location over the limit gets a 503 until UTC midnight.

Privacy

  • The route file is read in your browser and never sent anywhere; the page only sends its start point.
  • The location is rounded to 2 decimals before it leaves your device.
  • "Near you" links to OpenStreetMap with that rounded location, so you can check it; nothing goes there unless you open the link.
  • Pace, hours and the other settings are kept in your browser's storage; the route and location are not.
  • A route shared to the page waits briefly in your browser's cache; it is deleted as soon as the page opens it, and never sent.
  • The service receives the coordinates you send.
  • The service needs no token and knows nothing about your training. The TRMNL Running Board calls Tredict from inside TRMNL, so its token never comes here.
  • An Authorization or Cookie header is removed by nginx and again by the app, and is never read, logged or stored.
  • Plausible counts page views without cookies, and which settings, dialogs and links are used, by name only: never what you set, the route or the location. Plausible says it stores no IP addresses.
  • Weather is requested from Open-Meteo with coordinates rounded to 2 decimals.
  • The cache holds coordinates rounded to 2 decimals for about 3 hours. Data older than 3 hours is never served.
  • The count of distinct locations is stored as salted hashes, with the key kept only in memory and replaced daily, not written to disk or logs.
  • Logs hold known paths, coordinates rounded to 1 decimal and the User-Agent. Logs go to Better Stack.
  • Logs hold a client ID that is salted and changes every day, instead of an IP address.
